# 3. Data and Method

## 3.1 Market data
Daily NAV for SBI Pension Funds Tier I schemes E, C and G (2009-05-05 to 2026-08-10, npsnav.in, CC BY-NC 4.0)
converted to monthly simple returns. The fund's first calendar month (a flat-NAV launch period) is excluded,
leaving 207 months. Return paths are generated by a moving-block bootstrap (12-month blocks) that preserves
cross-asset correlation and regime structure.

## 3.2 Synthetic subscribers (PLFS-calibrated)
Three cohorts - regular salaried, casual labour, self-employed - with population shares, mean monthly
income and between-cell dispersion transcribed from PLFS Annual Report 2023-24 (Statements 6, 7, 10-12, 14).
The cells give a measured CV per cohort; an assumed individual-dispersion multiplier maps it to a target
individual CV, ranking cohorts into low/medium/high volatility tiers. Each subscriber's income is a
lognormal baseline draw (cross-sectional share of the CV) times an AR(1) log-shock process (temporal share),
a growth trend, seasonal factors for agriculture/construction, and Bernoulli disruption months anchored to the
PLFS unemployment rate. Contribution = propensity x max(0, income - subsistence floor), zero in a share of
disruption months. 2,000 subscribers x 432 months (ages 25-60).

## 3.3 Policy class and PFRDA baselines
PFRDA LC25/LC50/LC75 age tables are transcribed and used as baselines. Candidate policies are three-parameter
glide paths (start equity, end equity, inflection age), linearly de-risking to age 55 and flat after, with a
fixed 35/65 corporate-debt/gov-securities split of the remainder.

## 3.4 Outcomes
For each (market path, subscriber): terminal corpus; **intra-horizon shortfall** (corpus plus withdrawn cash
below 90% of cumulative contributions at any month once contributions exceed Rs 1,000); **XIRR** (money-weighted
return via vectorized Newton solver, validated against closed-form cases); and, optionally, a **liquidity
channel**: in each disruption month a subscriber withdraws 10% of the corpus with probability p.
Success is P(terminal corpus >= the scenario's LC50 median for that tier).

## 3.5 Risk-budget optimization and tests
Every candidate is simulated once per tier with common random numbers. The risk-budget policy maximizes success
subject to P(shortfall) <= epsilon; Pareto frontiers are the non-dominated candidates. The tier-blind benchmark
maximizes the subscriber-weighted average objective over all tiers. Selection uses seed 1 (100 trials), reporting
uses seed 2 (200 trials). The key test is the paired success gain of the tier-conditioned over the tier-blind
policy with a bootstrap CI over market trials. Robustness: (a) equity-premium haircut subtracted from every
bootstrapped equity month; (b) withdrawal probabilities; (c) perturbations of disruption rate, propensity,
dispersion and subsistence floor.

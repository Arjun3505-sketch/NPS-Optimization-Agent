# Abstract

India's NPS Auto Choice allocates by age alone. We ask whether conditioning the glide path on income
volatility would better serve the informal workers (78% of the workforce) whose contributions are irregular.
Because no subscriber-level NPS data are public, we combine real NPS NAV history (SBI Tier I, 2009-2026,
block bootstrap) with a synthetic population calibrated to PLFS 2023-24, and evaluate glide paths on terminal
wealth, intra-horizon shortfall, money-weighted return and a disruption-triggered withdrawal channel.
Income volatility sharply lowers and disperses accumulation (informal cohorts skip ~10% of contribution
months versus 0.07%), but it does not change the optimal allocation: under every objective, risk budget
and withdrawal setting tested, the optimal glide path is identical across volatility tiers, and a tier-blind
policy is statistically indistinguishable from a tier-conditioned one (0 of 54 robustness cells with a CI
excluding zero). The optimum is instead governed by the equity premium over government securities, flipping
from maximum to minimum equity at a premium of about 0-1% per year. We conclude that income-volatility
conditioning of the glide path is not where informal-worker outcomes are won or lost in this model; policy
effort is better directed at contribution regularity and liquidity design. We release the pipeline, a
risk-budget recommender, and all labelled assumptions.
